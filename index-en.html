Research background

Bring the intended grade back to the original

Two gray regions can call for different treatments. The sky may need a cooler tint, the sunlit rock a warmer one, and the mist between them a gradual transition. A useful color mapping depends on pixel color, what is in the scene, and where it appears.

Why use diffusion-based editing for color grading?

In our tests, Image 2.5 used a style image or written instructions to create a grading draft for the original scene. Conventional global color mappings tend to treat similar input colors alike. A diffusion model can use the subject, materials, and lighting to decide what to adjust, by how much, and how the changes should blend across regions.

An LLM operating curves, color wheels, and masks must break a complex local grade into separate adjustments and coordinate them. A diffusion editor can handle the lit rock, shaded ridgeline, and mist passing between them in one draft. The draft captures how color and tone should change together across the scene.

Grade the original from a same-scene reference

Generated references have lower resolution than high-megapixel originals. Even when the compositions are close, the model can redraw rock texture, clouds, or small edges and shift them slightly. We learn the grade from corresponding locations in the reference and original, then render at full resolution using the original pixels, coordinates, and neighborhoods.

Why we started with INRetouch

We compared INRetouch, SM-LUT, BGU, PETAL, and other methods. INRetouch gave the best overall results on the strong local grades that were the focus of this study, so we chose it as our starting point. It learns from one same-scene pair using color, position, and neighborhood information. We focused our changes on color representation, mapping stability, and the retention of fine texture.

Research method

From style inspiration to a full-resolution result

An external image model follows the original composition to produce a same-scene reference that approximates the style image’s color and tone. The transfer network learns a mapping from corresponding locations and applies it at the original photo’s resolution.

Lab residual modeling

Fit on a same-scene pair resized to 512 pixels on the long edge. The network encodes normalized CIELAB color and image-wide coordinates separately. A 3 × 3 depthwise convolution adds neighborhood context. The network predicts the Lab residual to add to the original, with separate loss terms for lightness and chroma. Each full-resolution tile uses image-wide coordinates and neighboring pixels across tile boundaries.

Mapping response regularization

Make two additional training inputs with small positive and negative neutral perturbations in RGB. Run the original and both probes through the same network. Use finite differences to constrain local curvature and gain, reducing abrupt transitions and reversals in tonal order.

Local correction and difference reconstruction

Fit a spatially smooth bilinear RGB offset field to remaining color differences at reference scale. Apply the offset at full resolution and run local Laplacian filtering before and after correction. Add the difference between the two filter outputs back to the base color match.
